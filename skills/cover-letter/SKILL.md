---
name: cover-letter
description: Write a submission or resubmission cover letter from the manuscript and the target journal.
---

Use this when the collaborator needs a cover letter.

1. Ask for the target journal, the article type, the editor's name if known, and whether this is a first submission, a revision, a resubmission after rejection, or a transfer from another journal. Do not guess a journal's scope or requirements; if the collaborator points to the journal's author guidelines, read them with fetch_url, and note anything they require the letter to contain (word count, suggested or opposed reviewers, a statement on prior publication or preprints, a special issue name).
2. Read the abstract, introduction and conclusion of the manuscript to find the question, the main result and why it matters. The letter is addressed to an editor deciding whether to send the paper for review, not to a specialist referee: say the result in plain terms.
3. Draft one page, in this order:
   - the salutation, to the named editor if there is one, otherwise to the editor or editors of the journal;
   - what is submitted: title, article type, and the special issue or section if any;
   - the question and the main finding in two or three sentences, with the key number if there is one;
   - why it fits this journal's readership, in one or two sentences grounded in the scope the guidelines state or in related work the journal has published that the manuscript cites; do not make up prior articles;
   - the statements the collaborator confirms: not published or under consideration elsewhere, all authors approved the submission, conflicts of interest, funding, ethics approval and consent where the study needs them, data and code availability, and any preprint with its identifier;
   - the corresponding author's name, affiliation and contact details.
4. Leave a clearly marked placeholder for any statement or detail you cannot confirm from the project; never assert one on the authors' behalf. List the placeholders for the collaborator at the end.
5. Suggested or opposed reviewers are included only when the journal asks for them and the collaborator supplies the names; do not propose people yourself.
6. For a revision, replace the pitch with one paragraph summarising the main changes and refer to the response letter. For a resubmission after rejection or a transfer, say so openly, give the earlier manuscript number if the collaborator has it, and say what changed since.
7. Claims must match the manuscript. Do not add superlatives or novelty claims the paper does not make, and do not repeat the abstract sentence for sentence.
8. Write it as a separate file in the project's language and compile if it is LaTeX; reuse the project's letter template if there is one. Keep it to one page unless the journal requires more.
