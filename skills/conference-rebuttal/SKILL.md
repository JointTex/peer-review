---
name: conference-rebuttal
description: Write a conference rebuttal or author response within the venue length limit, answering the concerns the decision turns on first.
---

Use this for an author response at a conference or any venue with a short, length-limited rebuttal, where a full point-by-point letter does not fit.

1. Get the reviews with their scores and confidence, and the meta-review if there is one. Ask for them if they are not in the conversation or the project. Never guess what a reviewer wrote.
2. Ask for the rules of this venue and this year, or read the author instructions with fetch_url if the collaborator gives the address. Do not assume them, since they change between venues and years: the limit and its unit (characters, words, or one PDF page), whether it is one shared response or one per reviewer, whether new results, figures, tables or links are allowed, whether the paper itself may be revised during the discussion, and what anonymity requires.
3. Read the parts of the paper the reviews are about. Check each claimed weakness against the paper: already answered in the paper (say where), a real gap, or a misunderstanding the writing invites.
4. Rank the concerns. First those that several reviewers share and those raised by the reviewer whose score could move; then factual errors about the paper; then the rest. A concern that cannot be answered is acknowledged briefly, not ignored and not argued around.
5. Agree with the collaborator on the answer to each ranked concern. New numbers come only from them or from the project: never invent a result, and never promise one for the final version unless they say it will be there. Where a result is still running, leave a marked placeholder.
6. Draft:
   - an opening of one or two lines at most, then straight to the substance;
   - shared concerns first under a short heading, each answered once, then per-reviewer answers labelled by the reviewer identifiers the venue uses;
   - each answer leads with the conclusion (the number, the location in the paper, the correction), then the reason, in two to four sentences;
   - a compact table for new results when tables are allowed, with the same metric and setting as the paper so they can be compared;
   - a concrete statement of what changes in the paper for each accepted point.
7. Keep it anonymous where the venue requires it: no author names, no institution, no link that identifies the authors.
8. Count the length the way the venue does and report the count. For a character limit count the text as it will be pasted, including markup and spaces; for a page limit compile it in the venue's rebuttal template. If it is over, cut in this order: thanks and restated praise, restated reviewer text, minor points, then hedging.
9. Tone: direct and specific. Do not tell a reviewer they are wrong without the evidence beside it, and do not call a point a misunderstanding when the paper was unclear; say the text will be clarified and how.
10. Deliver the response as its own file in the format the submission system takes (plain text, Markdown or LaTeX), with a list of placeholders and of concerns left unanswered and why.
